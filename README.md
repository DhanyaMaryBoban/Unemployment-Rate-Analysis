# UNEMPLOYMENT-RATE-ANALYSIS

## 📖 Overview
The study analyses unemployment trend in India during the period of 2018 to 2024 including time, gender, age and geography. The study explores which states consistently face the highest or lowest unemployment, quarterly variations, gender and youth unemployment burdens, and the impact of the pandemic years on joblessness, while also identifying states that remain above or below the national average.

## 🎯 Important Questions 
- Which states have the highest and lowest unemployment rate?
- Show the unemployment trend in India during the period 2018-2024.
- Which quarter (Q1–Q4) consistently shows the highest or lowest average unemployment rate across 2018–2024?
- Which gender face the unemployment burden the most?
- Is youth unemployment (15-29 years) significantly higher than other age groups?
- In the state with the highest unemployment rate, which gender and age group face the greatest unemployment burden?
- How did quarterly unemployment rates evolve between 2018 and 2024, particularly in response to the pandemic spike of 2019–2021, and what observable signs of recovery can be noted in the subsequent years?
- Which states consistently show unemployment rates above or below the national average?

## 🔍 Key Findings
- Kerala faced the highest unemployment issue among all states, followed by Rajasthan and Jammu & Kashmir. Gujarat has the lowest,followed by West Bengal and Karnataka.
- Peak unemployment occurred between 2019–2021, especially in 2020, due to COVID-19. Rates declined below 10% by 2024.
- April–June quarter shows the highest unemployment (19.41%), while January–March shows the lowest (13.84%).
- Female unemployment (18.47%) is significantly higher than male (12.28%).
- Ages 15–29 face the highest unemployment (25.15%), far above the overall average (10%).
- In Kerala, youth females (15–29) face the greatest unemployment burden.
- Gujarat, Karnataka, West Bengal, Delhi, Haryana, Punjab, and Bihar consistently perform better than average, while Kerala, Telangana, Rajasthan, J&K, Uttarakhand, and Andhra Pradesh perform worse.

## ✅ Recommendations
- The state which face servere and consistent unemployment issue should focus on training people in new skills and bringing in different industries. In states with low unemployment, copy the successful programs and policies that already work there.
-  It is essential to build backup support for workers like unemployment benefits, food security for workers during crises and fast programs like training, apprenticeships to help them re-enter jobs, so future shocks don't cause long lasting unemployment.
-  Introduce seasonal employment programs (public works, skill training) during high-unemployment quarters.
-  Expand women-focused employment initiatives — childcare support, flexible work policies, and targeted skill training.
-  Strengthen vocational training, apprenticeships, and entrepreneurship programs for young people.
-  Launch targeted interventions in Kerala — women’s skill development, job placement schemes, and schemes to increase women entrepreneurship.
-  States should learn from each other, don't rely on single industry rather open to new industries and tackle the root causes of unemployment such as skill mismatches, lack of infrastructure, seasonal job gaps or overdependence on government jobs in regions that are struggling.
